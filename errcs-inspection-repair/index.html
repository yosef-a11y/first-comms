Annual ERRCS inspection, and what to do when one fails

Once an ERRCS or BDA system is in, it has to keep working — and keep proving it works. That means a test every year, batteries that hold their charge, and records the fire official can ask for at any time.

Annual testing

Required every year once a system is installed, and it is the building owner’s obligation, not the installer’s. We do the yearly test and keep the records ready for the fire official.

Battery checks

Systems are generally required to run on battery through a power loss. Batteries age; we check them at the yearly test and replace them when they are due.

Fault alarms

The annunciator panel reports antenna failure, amplifier failure and low battery. If yours is showing a fault, the system is telling you it may not work when it is needed.

Failed inspection

If the building or the system did not pass

A fail is a measurement, not a verdict. The same grid data that shows where the building fell short shows what it would take to fix it.

Retest to find the fault

We measure the building against the grid so the fail can be located — a dead stairwell, a basement, a floor — rather than argued about.

Repair or re-tune

A failing antenna, amplifier or battery is replaced; a channelised system whose county channels have changed may need re-tuning rather than new equipment.

Retest and document

The repaired system is tested against the same grid, and you get a written result you can hand back to the fire official.
